Best PhD Thesis Award – Austrian Physical Society

September 2023 | Austria

I was very happy to receive one of the three **Best PhD Thesis Awards from the Austrian Physical Society (ÖPG)**, recognizing doctoral theses from the **2021–2023 period**. The award was presented by the president of the ÖPG in Basel, Switzerland.

The ÖPG has a long tradition of supporting physics research, with its members including two recent Nobel laureates in Physics: Anton Zeilinger (2022) and Ferenc Krausz (2023).

My thesis, *“Complex oxide surfaces studied at the atomic scale with AFM/STM and DFT”*, is the result of **four and a half years of research**, countless experiments, discussions, challenges, and, of course, some unexpected discoveries along the way. It was a long but incredibly rewarding endeavour, and it was especially nice to see all that work recognized in this way.

A big thank you goes to my supervisors, **Prof. Martin Setvín and Prof. Ulrike Diebold**, as well as everyone else involved in my supervision over the years. Their guidance, support, and many scientific discussions played an important role in shaping both the thesis and my development as a researcher. I’m also grateful to all the colleagues and collaborators who contributed to the work.For anyone interested in the research, the full PhD thesis is available [here](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/18545).

As part of the award ceremony, I also had the opportunity to **give a talk about my research at the "Young Minds" symposium**, addressing a younger audience that included high school and graduate students. It was a particularly nice part of the event, and I hope I managed to spark some curiosity and perhaps inspire a few future researchers to pursue science themselves.

It was a lovely occasion to receive the award in Basel and a nice way to mark the end of an important chapter of my scientific career.
