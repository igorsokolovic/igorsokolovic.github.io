## Paul Schöngrundner Successfully Completes His Master's Thesis!

*August 2024 | Exploring crystalline silicon oxide on SiC surfaces*

After successfully completing his Bachelor's thesis in 2023, **Paul Schöngrundner returned to work with me for his Master's research**, which I once again had the pleasure of co-supervising. In August 2024, he successfully completed his thesis, *"Search for crystalline SiO<sub>2</sub> on the wet chemically treated 6H-SiC(0001) surface."*

The project started with an intriguing observation: a silicon carbide (SiC) surface prepared through wet-chemical treatment appeared to host a thin, crystalline silicon oxide (SiO<sub>2</sub>) overlayer. Such a structure could provide an interesting model system for studying surface chemistry, provided we could reliably reproduce and control it.

Over **six months of experimental work**, Paul took on the challenge of understanding how different chemical and thermal treatments influence the formation of this oxide layer. Combining **X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), atomic force microscopy (AFM), noncontact AFM (nc-AFM), and low-energy electron diffraction (LEED)**, he systematically investigated different preparation procedures.

The results turned out to be more complicated, and more interesting, than initially expected. While we developed improved methods for obtaining cleaner SiC surfaces, we also discovered that **a cleaner surface does not necessarily produce the desired crystalline oxide**. By carefully revisiting the original preparation conditions, Paul managed to recover the ordered surface structure, revealing some unexpected connections between surface chemistry and oxide crystallization.

I want to give a particularly big thank you to **Paul for his dedication, persistence, and the enormous amount of careful experimental work and data analysis** throughout the project. I especially appreciated our many thorough scientific discussions, his open-minded approach to unexpected results, and his willingness to come up with and test new ideas. Several of these ideas helped us move the research forward, and it was genuinely enjoyable to tackle these challenges together.

It was a real pleasure to work with Paul again and to see his development as a researcher over these two projects. Congratulations on successfully completing your Master's degree, Paul!

For anyone interested in the details, the full thesis is publicly available through [TU Wien's repository](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/204132).
