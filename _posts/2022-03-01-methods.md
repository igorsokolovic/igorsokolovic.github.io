How can we see individual atoms, identify the chemical composition of a surface, or understand its electronic structure? Modern surface science relies on a combination of experimental and theoretical techniques, each revealing a different piece of the puzzle.

During my PhD, I spent quite some time exploring the principles behind these methods and documenting them in my thesis. In particular, I wrote detailed explanations of scanning tunneling microscopy (STM), noncontact atomic force microscopy (nc-AFM), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), low-energy electron diffraction (LEED), and density functional theory (DFT).

The sections cover not only the physical principles behind these methods, but also how they are used in practice, what information we can extract from them, and some of their limitations.

I've included these explanations in the introductory chapters of my [PhD Thesis](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/18545). If you're a student getting started in surface science, or simply curious about how we investigate materials at the atomic scale, I hope you'll find them useful!
