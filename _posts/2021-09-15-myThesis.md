## PhD Defense at TU Wien

On **15 September 2021**, I successfully defended my PhD thesis at **TU Wien**, bringing four and a half years of research, experiments, calculations, and countless hours spent thinking about atoms to a very happy conclusion!

My thesis, *"Complex oxide surfaces studied at the atomic scale with AFM/STM and DFT"*, was carried out under the supervision of **Prof. Martin Setvín and Prof. Ulrike Diebold**. The defense committee included **Prof. Leo Gross** from IBM Research in Zurich and **Prof. Zdeněk Dohnálek** from Pacific Northwest National Laboratory in the USA. It was a pleasure to present and discuss my work with such distinguished scientists.

The research focused on understanding the physics and chemistry of oxide surfaces at the atomic scale, combining **atomic force microscopy (AFM), scanning tunneling microscopy (STM), and density functional theory (DFT)**. Much of the work revolved around two fascinating materials, TiO<sub>2</sub> and SrTiO<sub>3</sub>, exploring everything from the interaction of individual molecules with surfaces to unusual electronic properties and new ways of preparing atomically clean crystals.

Of course, the most valuable part of these years was everything I learned from the people around me.

A special thank you to **Martin Setvín** for teaching me the intricacies of AFM and STM and guiding me through the experimental side of surface physics. To **Michael Schmid**, who always had good advice, particularly when the problems seemed most difficult. To **Michele Reticcioli and Cesare Franchini** for introducing me to the world of DFT and helping me understand the theory behind our experiments. And, of course, to **Ulrike Diebold**, who first introduced me to surface science and made sure I would remain fascinated by this field for years to come.

I'm also grateful to all the colleagues, collaborators, and friends who made these four and a half years such an enjoyable experience.

For anyone curious about the research, or interested in the experimental and theoretical techniques behind it, the full thesis is freely available [here](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/18545).

Looking back, it was quite a journey, and certainly one that shaped the way I think about and approach science. A great chapter to finish, and an exciting one to build upon!
